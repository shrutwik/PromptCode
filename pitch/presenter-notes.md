PromptCode is built around the decision that comes before it: who should we hire?

## Likely judge questions

**Why not HackerRank or CodeSignal?** Both offer AI assessments. PromptCode focuses on operational evidence for AI workflows; buyer preference for that depth still needs to be demonstrated. The table compares documented evaluation approaches; it does not claim that competitors lack every related feature or that our hiring outcomes are already proven superior.

**Does the score predict a good employee?** Not yet established. The pilot should compare scores with independent work reviews, test repeatability and subgroup performance where data permits, then study job outcomes with appropriate consent. Keep humans responsible for hiring decisions.

**Do you have customers?** No verified customer or revenue figures were provided for this pitch. Pilot counts are goals, and sample results are synthetic. Present actual traction only when you can substantiate it.

**How is the market sized?** Polaris estimates the broad candidate-skills-assessment category at $3.18B in 2025. Census counts 9,467 U.S. computer-services firms with 20–199 employees. At the proposed $12K annual price the entry-segment revenue ceiling is $113.604M; at 25% qualification it is $28.401M. Qualification and price remain assumptions. See research.md for the nine source bins.

**What if evaluation is more expensive?** At $5 per assessment, 100 assessments plus $50 hosting/support cost $550 per $1,000 account, producing 45% gross margin before fixed costs. Adjust quotas and pricing after measuring real usage.

**How do you stop gaming?** Hidden tasks, varied inputs, repeated runs, and baseline comparisons make some shortcuts harder. No assessment is claimed to be cheat-proof.

**Why should the business retain customers after hiring slows?** Team upskilling is the expansion hypothesis, not proven retention. First measure repeat usage and paid renewals in the hiring segment.

**What is the venture-scale outcome?** Expand through reusable role assessments, recurring team development, and partner distribution. Talent-assessment or developer-tool vendors could be potential strategic acquirers, but there is no buyer interest or exit valuation claim.
