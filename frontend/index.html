Before you start

A few things
to know.

What you’ll work on, how the assistant fits in, and what you can learn from each session.

What will I work on?

You’ll work on realistic bugs and feature tasks in existing codebases. Examples include an invoice with the wrong status, an order missing its hold reason, a stale notification feed, and a CSV merge that doubles quantities.

Each session gives you a task, an editor, tests, and an AI assistant. Explore the code, investigate the cause, and make a focused change while preserving existing behavior. It’s practice for developers who want to get better at working through unfamiliar projects and engineering interviews with AI in the loop.

Can I use an AI assistant?

Yes. The workspace includes an assistant, and using it is part of the practice. Ask for help understanding unfamiliar code, exploring possible causes, or drafting a fix.

You decide what to accept, edit, or reject. Review suggestions against the task and the surrounding code, then run tests and inspect the results. The practice is about combining AI assistance with your own engineering judgment.

What does the score measure?

The report gives feedback on how you investigate the code, verify changes, use AI with judgment, and complete the task. The landing-page score is an illustrative example out of 100. Passing tests is one signal; your approach and decisions also matter.

After submitting, you can review your code changes and a timeline of recorded activity. When available, follow-up questions ask you to explain your decisions. Use the feedback to reflect on your workflow and identify what to improve in your next session. This is practice feedback, not a hiring decision or a guarantee of interview performance.

How do I get started?

Create an account or sign in, browse the project library, and choose a challenge. Anyone can create an account; no invite is needed.

Read the task and explore the code before changing it. Use the assistant when it helps, make your changes, and run the tests. Inspect failures, check that the fix addresses the problem, and submit your work when you’re ready to review the result.
